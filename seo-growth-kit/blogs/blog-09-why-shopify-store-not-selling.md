# Why Your Shopify Store Isn't Selling: 21 Conversion Killers We Fix Most Often

**Stores don't sell for four reasons: the right people don't arrive (traffic quality), the store doesn't load or persuade (speed/UX), trust is missing (proof and policy), or checkout fights the buyer (payment and friction).** Below are the 21 specific killers we encounter most in Indian store audits, grouped by where they strike — with the fix for each.

Run your store against this list before spending another rupee on ads; fixing a killer multiplies every future rupee too.

## A. Traffic problems (killers 1–4)

| # | Killer | The tell | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You're buying cheap clicks, not buyers | High sessions, 5-second engagement, zero add-to-carts | Tighten ad targeting and creative-message match; judge traffic on add-to-cart rate, not CPC |
| 2 | Broad "awareness" campaigns with sale expectations | Mostly new visitors hit cold pages | Build the funnel: cold → value/product education → retargeting with proof |
| 3 | Dependence on one channel | One ad account = one point of failure | Layer email/WhatsApp lists, SEO content and marketplaces |
| 4 | Visitors from the wrong geography/device | Sales from cities you don't ship to fast | Geo-target ads to serviceable PIN zones; show delivery promise by region |

## B. Speed & mobile (killers 5–8)

| # | Killer | The tell | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 6+ second mobile load on Indian 4G | PageSpeed mobile LCP >2.5–3s | Compress images, kill unused apps/scripts, upgrade theme |
| 6 | App bloat | 20+ apps, duplicated scripts | Quarterly app audit; replace three apps with one where possible |
| 7 | Popup ambush | Newsletter popup before content loads | Delay popups; exit-intent over entry-intent |
| 8 | Hero banner the size of a cricket field | Buyer scrolls twice to see a product | Product-visible hero or a fast merchandised grid above the fold |

## C. Trust & persuasion (killers 9–14)

| # | Killer | The tell | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | No reviews or ratings anywhere | Zero social proof on product pages | Review app + post-delivery review requests (WhatsApp works well) |
| 10 | Stock-photo brand | No real photos, faces, or packaging shots | Founder/user-generated content; real unboxing photos |
| 11 | Vague or punitive policies | "No returns" buried in footer | Clear, generous, *visible* returns — returns clarity lifts conversion more than it costs |
| 12 | Missing delivery promise | Buyer wonders "will it reach me? When?" | Delivery estimate on product page by PIN; COD availability stated plainly |
| 13 | Weak product pages | Supplier text, 2 images, no video | 5+ images (incl. scale/use), short video, benefits-led copy, sizing help |
| 14 | No human behind the brand | About page is two sentences | Founders, process, workshop/kitchen/factory photos — people buy from people |

## D. Offer & pricing (killers 15–17)

| # | Killer | The tell | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | Price with no framing | Same product cheaper on marketplaces | Bundle value (kits), first-order offers, loyalty framing — compete on offer architecture, not rupee-matching |
| 16 | Flat, always-on discount | Trained to wait for sales | Calendar your offers; reward lists/community with early access instead of blanket % |
| 17 | Free-shipping threshold too low or absent | AOV stuck | Set threshold just above current AOV; show progress bar in cart |

## E. Checkout & payment (killers 18–21)

| # | Killer | The tell | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | Payment options mismatched to India | Card-first checkout | UPI first, then wallets/COD; test every gateway path monthly |
| 19 | Forced account creation | Drop-off at register | Guest checkout enabled by default |
| 20 | Surprise costs at last step | Shipping revealed at payment | Show shipping/fee expectations on product/cart; keep COD fee honest but early |
| 21 | No recovery system | Carts vanish silently | WhatsApp + email cart recovery within the hour; COD-order confirmation flow to kill junk before dispatch |

## The audit order we use

1. **Analytics sanity** (is tracking even right?) → 2. **Mobile speed** → 3. **Mobile purchase of your own product, honestly** → 4. **Trust inventory** (would a stranger give you ₹1,999?) → 5. **Checkout walkthrough on 4G** → 6. **Offer review** → 7. **Traffic quality by source**.

This sequence finds the constraint in that order — because fixing persuasion while checkout is broken is theatre.

## FAQs

### My store gets traffic but no sales — where do I start?
Run the trust and checkout sections first (killers 9–21). In Indian audits, the most common revenue-releasing fixes are delivery-promise clarity, UPI/COD checkout ordering, and cart recovery automation — not redesigns.

### How much can conversion rate realistically improve?
Meaningful gains are routine when a store starts from a weak baseline — doubling conversion from ~0.5% to ~1% is common with disciplined fixes. Guarantees of specific multipliers are marketing, not math; your catalogue, price point and traffic mix set the ceiling.

### Do I need a redesign to fix conversions?
Rarely. Most of the 21 killers are content, policy, speed and flow fixes inside your existing theme. Redesign when the theme itself is the speed or mobile bottleneck — not as a first move.

### Are discounts the answer to low sales?
Usually the most expensive wrong answer. Fix conversion fundamentals first; otherwise discounts just reprice the traffic you were already converting.

### How do I know which killer is costing me the most?
Look at analytics drop-off: where do sessions die — landing (speed/message), product (trust/offer), or checkout (payment/friction)? The biggest cliff is your first project.

## Conclusion

A store that isn't selling is a system with one binding constraint — speed, trust, offer or checkout. Find it, fix it, then feed it traffic. The 21 killers above cover what we find in nine of ten Indian store audits; your store has two or three of them working overtime right now.
