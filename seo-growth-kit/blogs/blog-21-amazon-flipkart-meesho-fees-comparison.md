# Amazon vs Flipkart vs Meesho: What Sellers Actually Pay in India (Fee Comparison)

**The honest comparison isn't commission rates — it's all-in cost per delivered order on *your* price band and category. Amazon and Flipkart stack referral commission + closing fees + shipping + returns handling; Meesho runs a famously low-commission, seller-ships-default model that shifts cost into your logistics and higher return rates. The winner depends on your AOV, category return rates and who controls shipping.**

Fee structures change with marketplaces' periodic revisions — always verify current rate cards in seller panels. What doesn't change is the *method* below for computing your real margin.

## The fee stack, component by component

| Fee component | Amazon.in | Flipkart | Meesho |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Referral/commission** | Category-based % (typically single digits to mid-teens; varies by category and price band) | Similar category-based structure | Famously 0% commission on most categories (its core seller pitch) |
| **Closing/fix fee** | Price-band based slabs | Price-band based slabs | Low/zero on many slabs (structure has shifted over time — verify current) |
| **Shipping** | Weight-based; easy-ship/FBA options | Weight-based/slab | Default seller-arranged; Meesho-run options evolving |
| **Returns handling** | Charged on customer-return RTO events | Similar | Significant practical cost: apparel/general returns run high; you bear reverse logistics in most setups |
| **Ads (de facto fee)** | Competitive CPCs in most categories | Competitive CPCs | Cheaper clicks, lower intent quality historically |
| **Settlement/payout** | Weekly cycles typical | Weekly cycles typical | Frequent/quick payouts (a real cash-flow plus) |

*(Structural comparison as of writing; exact percentages live in each panel's fee calculator — recheck quarterly.)*

## The calculation that actually decides (do this per SKU)

**Real margin = Selling price − COGS − (referral % + closing + shipping − GST input credits) − expected return cost (return rate × reverse-fee + repackaging/loss) − ads per order**

Worked example (illustrative numbers — substitute your own):
- Price ₹799 apparel item, COGS ₹300
- Referral ~₹100–130 · closing ~₹20–40 · shipping ~₹60–90 (weight/zone)
- Returns: 20% return rate × (reverse shipping ~₹70 + 10% absolute loss) ≈ ₹30–40 effective per order
- Ads: ₹50 per order blended
- **All-in platform cost ≈ ₹260–₹340 → contribution before overhead ≈ ₹160–₹240/order (~20–30%)**

Run that same SKU across all three marketplaces with *their* current slabs and *your* return history per platform — the ranking is often not what commission headlines suggest.

## Where each marketplace genuinely wins

**Amazon.in**
- Premium-leaning, higher-AOV audiences; strongest Prime/logistics in metros
- Best tooling (Brand Analytics, A/B testing, brand store) — you can *work* the system
- Costs more per order in many categories; demands operational discipline

**Flipkart**
- Strong tier-2/3 reach, big-event days (Big Billion Days) that move serious volume
- Categories like mobiles/fashion/apparel have deep demand
- Similar fee stack to Amazon; event participation has real prep costs (deals, stock)

**Meesho**
- 0% commission model = a genuine margin edge on low-ticket items IF your reverse logistics are tight
- Massive tier-2/3/value-fashion audience; quick payouts help cash flow
- You carry shipping complexity and (category-dependent) higher return behaviour — the fee you "don't pay" reappears as logistics and returns management

## Strategy: run a portfolio, not a favourite

Most healthy Indian sellers treat marketplaces as a portfolio:
1. **Amazon/Flipkart** for premium SKUs and brand-search defence, where tooling and AOV justify fees
2. **Meesho** for value SKUs and tier-2/3 demand where its zero-commission math wins
3. **Your own Shopify store** as the margin-keeper — marketplaces pay for discovery; the store captures the relationship (email/WhatsApp list, full-margin repeat purchases)

And always: marketplace revenue should teach you which products deserve D2C investment, not replace D2C entirely.

## Fee-optimisation checklist (legal and boring, like all good margin work)

- Verify every SKU's category mapping (miscategorisation = paying wrong commission)
- Size-band your shipping: packaging that drops a weight slab pays you every order
- Track return rates by SKU/platform; delist or re-merchandise chronic returners (returns are the hidden commission)
- Use each panel's fee calculator before pricing changes — never price from gross margin
- Claim input GST correctly on marketplace fees; reconcile settlements monthly (fee errors are common and refundable if caught)
- Bid ads only where contribution margin survives the fee stack

## FAQs

### Which marketplace is cheapest for sellers in India?
On commission alone, Meesho. On all-in cost per *delivered, kept* order — it depends on your category's return rates and who arranges shipping. Compute per SKU with the formula above; results flip constantly by category.

### Are marketplace fees GST-taxable in India?
Marketplace fees (commission, closing, shipping charged by the platform) attract GST, and sellers with proper registration typically claim input credit — which is why reconciling fee invoices monthly matters for real margin.

### Do returns really change the marketplace comparison that much?
On apparel and footwear, massively: a 20–30% return rate means the reverse-logistics cost is effectively a second commission. Compare platforms on delivered-and-kept economics, never on shipped orders.

### Is FBA/easy-ship style logistics worth the cost?
Usually yes for fast-moving, standard-size SKUs where delivery speed drives conversion (and Prime-style badges matter); questionable for oversized, slow-moving or fragile items where storage and handling fees eat the margin. Model both per SKU.

### How much should I spend on marketplace ads?
Treat ads as part of the fee stack: start where contribution margin survives ads, harvest converting search terms, and cap spend per SKU by its true margin. Ads on negative-contribution SKUs are donations to the marketplace.

## Conclusion

There is no universally "cheapest" Indian marketplace — there's only your SKU-level, delivered-order margin on each platform, computed honestly and rechecked quarterly. Amazon buys tooling and premium reach, Flipkart buys event-driven volume, Meesho buys zero-commission access to value India — and your own store buys the future. Run the portfolio accordingly.
