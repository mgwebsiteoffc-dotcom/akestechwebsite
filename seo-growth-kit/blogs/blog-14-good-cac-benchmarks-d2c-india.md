# What Is a Good CAC for D2C Brands in India? Benchmarks + the Unit-Economics Formula

**There is no single "good" CAC number — a ₹900 CAC is excellent for a ₹4,000-AOV brand with repeat purchase and fatal for a ₹499-AOV one-off product. The honest benchmark is relational: CAC must fit inside contribution margin and pay back within a cycle you can fund. For most healthy Indian D2C brands, first-order contribution margin covers 30–100% of CAC, with repeat purchases carrying the rest of the payback.**

Here's the formula, the ranges, and the diagnostic for when CAC climbs.

## The formula (no ambiguity)

**CAC = total sales & marketing spend ÷ number of new customers acquired**

Decisions to make before you trust your own number:
- Include agency fees, creative production, tool costs? (Yes — they're real acquisition costs.)
- Attribute COD orders that later RTO? (No — count *confirmed, delivered, kept* customers.)
- New customers only, or all orders? (New customers — or repeat orders flatter the number into uselessness.)

## The ratio that actually decides survival

**LTV : CAC** — the lifetime value of a customer against what you paid for them. Common working standards in D2C:

- **Below 1:1** — you lose money on every customer; only fundable as a deliberate land-grab with deep pockets
- **~1.5–2:1** — fragile: any CPM rise or RTO spike flips you negative
- **3:1 or better** — comfortable zone for scaling spend
- **5:1+** — possibly *under*-investing in growth; if margins allow, raise acquisition spend

LTV for Indian D2C should be calculated on **contribution margin per order × expected orders**, not revenue — revenue-based LTV is how brands feel rich and die broke.

## Realistic ranges by pattern (directional, not gospel)

| Brand pattern | AOV | Workable CAC zone (first-order CM covering this) |
|---|---|---|
| Fashion/beauty one-and-done, ~60% GM | ₹800–₹2,000 | ₹400–₹900, needing 25%+ repeat rate to be comfortable |
| Consumables/supplements with subscription | ₹1,000–₹2,500 | ₹600–₹1,500 — repeat purchases carry payback |
| Electronics/home, considered purchase | ₹3,000–₹15,000 | ₹800–₹2,500 on strong first-order margin |
| Low-ticket impulse (<₹500) | ₹300–₹500 | Must land near ₹150–₹300 or the model needs bundles/AOV moves first |

Use these as sanity bands, not targets — your GM%, RTO% and category set the real boundaries. Anyone quoting universal CAC benchmarks in rupees without asking your AOV is selling comfort, not analysis.

## The diagnostic: CAC rising quarter over quarter — where's the leak?

1. **Creative fatigue** — same ads, decaying CTRs (most common; fix: new concepts, not new audiences)
2. **Tracking drift** — events breaking, optimization chasing the wrong signal (fix: event audit)
3. **COD mix shift** — more RTO-heavy orders inflating "conversions" (fix: verification flows, COD-adjusted reporting)
4. **Audience saturation** — frequency climbing on a narrow base (fix: broad + regional/language expansion)
5. **Offer erosion** — competitors matching your promos (fix: offer architecture, bundles, loyalty)
6. **Landing-page decay** — speed regression or message mismatch after "small" site changes (fix: LP discipline)

## How to *lower* CAC honestly (ranked by typical impact)

1. **Raise AOV** — bundles and free-shipping thresholds make the same CAC profitable
2. **Fix conversion fundamentals** — speed, trust, UPI/COD checkout order (a +0.3% CVR improvement is a CAC cut)
3. **Creative velocity** — the cheapest scale lever on Meta
4. **Retention flows** — repeat orders amortise every rupee of acquisition
5. **Regional expansion** — regional-language creatives often buy attention below metro prices
6. **Only then: bid/budget tinkering** — the lever everyone pulls first and that moves least

## FAQs

### Should CAC include COD RTO losses?
For decision-making, yes: a "customer" who refuses delivery isn't a customer. Track both raw CAC and kept-customer CAC; scale on the second.

### Is a 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio realistic in India?
Achievable in categories with genuine repeat behaviour; harder for one-and-done fashion where the model leans on first-order margin. Set the target from your category's repeat dynamics, not from a Twitter rule of thumb.

### Blended CAC vs paid CAC — which should I report?
Both, to different audiences: blended (all spend ÷ all new customers) for business health; paid-channel CAC for media decisions. Reporting only blended hides channel disasters; reporting only paid ignores organic's contribution.

### How do I compute LTV without fancy tools?
Average order value × gross margin % × orders per customer per year × average customer lifespan (be conservative: 1–2 years unless data says otherwise). Spreadsheet-grade and honest beats dashboard-grade and inflated.

### CAC is fine but profit isn't. What's breaking?
Look past acquisition: RTO%, gateway/delivery fees, discount depth, returns, and contribution margin per order. CAC is one hole in the boat — check the others before celebrating.

## Conclusion

"Good CAC" is not a number you copy — it's a fit between your margins, repeat behaviour and payback appetite. Compute it honestly (kept customers, all-in spend), track it as a ratio to contribution-margin LTV, and diagnose rises through the six-leak list before touching budgets.
