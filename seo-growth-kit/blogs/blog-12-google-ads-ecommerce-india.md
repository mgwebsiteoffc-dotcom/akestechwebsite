# Google Ads for Indian Ecommerce: Shopping, Performance Max & a Setup That Actually Works

**Google Ads works for Indian ecommerce when three things are true: your Merchant Center feed is genuinely good, your campaign structure separates brand from non-brand, and your tracking counts COD-adjusted reality. Get those right and even modest budgets compound; skip them and Performance Max will happily spend your money learning nothing.**

## The layer map (what each campaign type is for)

| Campaign | Job | When it earns its keep |
|---|---|---|
| **Branded Search** | Protect your name from marketplaces/competitors; cheap high-converting traffic | Always — from day one |
| **Standard Shopping** | Product-level control over bids, bids on real margin data | Catalogue ≤ a few hundred SKUs, or margin varies wildly across products |
| **Performance Max** | Automated reach across Shopping, Search, YouTube, Display, Gmail | Broad catalogues, enough conversion data to steer, quality assets provided |
| **Dynamic Search / generic Search** | Capture problem/category queries Shopping misses | Supplementary; tight keyword themes |
| **YouTube / Demand Gen** | Demand creation for visual categories | Once the capture engine is profitable |

## Step 1 — Fix the feed before touching campaigns

Your Shopping results are only as good as your Merchant Center data:

- **Titles built for search:** `Brand + Product Type + Key Attribute (colour/size/pack)` — not supplier strings
- **Clean categories and GTINs** where products have them
- **Multiple images per product**; lifestyle shot as secondary
- **Price and availability synced daily** — disapprovals from stale stock silently kill bestsellers
- **Shipping and GST-inclusive pricing configured for India** so the displayed price doesn't change at checkout

Feed audits routinely find 10–30% of a catalogue invisible due to disapprovals nobody checks. Check weekly.

## Step 2 — Structure campaigns by intent, not by convenience

1. **Branded Search campaign** — separate budget, separate reporting, phrase/exact match. This is defence: marketplaces bid on your name; the question is whether *you* do.
2. **Shopping/Performance Max for catalogue** — exclude branded terms from non-brand campaigns (brand negatives) or reporting becomes unreadable.
3. **Non-brand Search** — 3–5 tight ad groups of genuine buying queries ("buy running shoes online", "protein powder 1kg price"). Broad match + smart bidding can work here, but only with a negative-keyword list you actually maintain.
4. **PMax vs Standard decision:** start with Standard Shopping if SKU count is manageable and margins differ across products (you need product-level control). Move to PMax when you can feed it: strong titles, asset groups by category, audience signals, and enough conversion volume. PMax with a lazy feed is a black box with your money inside.

## Step 3 — Build the India-specific measurement layer

- **Conversion actions:** purchase (primary), begin-checkout/add-to-cart (secondary), WhatsApp/call clicks (primary *if* sales close there — many Indian orders do)
- **COD reality:** import COD-confirmed orders or value-adjust conversions so bids don't chase orders that will be refused at the door
- **Server-side/Conversions API-class tracking** where feasible; browser-only tracking under-counts iOS and cross-device paths
- **One attribution truth:** reconcile Google's reported conversions against your store's orders monthly — if the gap is large, fix measurement before scaling spend

## Step 4 — Budgets that make sense (starting out)

| Slice | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Branded Search | ~10–15% | Low CPC, protects your funnel's front door |
| Shopping/Standard | ~50–60% | The workhorse for product demand |
| Non-brand Search | ~15–20% | High-intent themes only; ruthless negatives |
| Testing/YouTube/Demand Gen | ~10–15% | Only after capture layers are stable |

Review weekly against contribution margin (post-COD, post-returns). Kill by rule, not by mood: e.g., any ad group 2× target CPA after fair spend gets paused, not "optimised" forever.

## The mistakes that burn Indian ecommerce accounts

1. **One PMax campaign for everything** — no asset-group structure, no brand exclusions, no idea what's working
2. **Feeds abandoned after launch** — disapprovals accumulate, prices drift, bestsellers vanish silently
3. **Brand and non-brand mixed** — inflated ROAS hiding non-brand losses
4. **No COD/RTO adjustment** — bidding up orders that return at the door
5. **Expecting Google to create demand** — it captures it; creation is creative's job (Meta, YouTube, content)

## FAQs

### Should I start with Performance Max or Standard Shopping?
Start Standard Shopping when you need product-level control (varied margins, smaller catalogue); graduate to PMax with structured asset groups once data accumulates. If you must pick one with a weak feed: fix the feed first — that decision matters more than the campaign type.

### How much budget for Google Ads in India to start?
Enough to buy meaningful data on your target CPA — commonly a few thousand rupees a day for small catalogues. Below that threshold, concentrate on branded + best-selling SKUs rather than spreading thin everywhere.

### Are Google Ads worth it for small Indian brands?
For capture demand — yes, particularly branded defence and Shopping on hero products. If nobody searches your category, spend on demand creation (Meta/YouTube/content) before forcing Google to manufacture interest.

### Why do my Google conversions not match my Shopify orders?
Normal causes: attribution windows, cross-device paths, iOS gaps, COD cancellations, and double-counted view-through conversions. Reconcile monthly on orders-and-margin, not platform-reported conversions.

### How do I stop marketplaces outranking me on my own brand name?
Run branded Search with sitelinks, structured snippets and your best offer; keep feed prices competitive; and build enough brand search volume (community, WhatsApp, packaging inserts with QR to site) that your own demand crowds them out.

## Conclusion

Google Ads for Indian ecommerce is a plumbing-first channel: feed, structure, tracking — then budget. Brands that romanticise it as a "set and forget" AI machine get outspent by brands that audit their feeds weekly and count real, COD-adjusted margin.
